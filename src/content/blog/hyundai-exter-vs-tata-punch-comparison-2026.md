## Exter vs Punch: Engine and Performance

When you're driving through the busy streets of Nizamabad or heading out towards the SRSP project, engine performance and fuel efficiency are key. Both the Hyundai Exter and Tata Punch offer petrol engine options, but their characteristics differ.

The Hyundai Exter comes with a 1.2-litre Kappa petrol engine, delivering 83 PS of power and 113.8 Nm of torque. It's known for its refinement and smooth power delivery, making city driving a breeze. You get options for both a 5-speed manual transmission and an AMT (Automated Manual Transmission), which is a huge plus for those who want clutch-free convenience in traffic. The Exter also offers a factory-fitted CNG option, which is a great cost-saver for high-mileage users.

The Tata Punch, on the other hand, is powered by a 1.2-litre Revotron petrol engine producing 88 PS and 115 Nm of torque. While slightly more powerful on paper, it can feel a bit more vocal than the Exter's engine. It also comes with 5-speed manual and AMT gearbox options. The Punch feels robust and capable, especially on broken patches of road, which is something many of our customers appreciate when driving in rural areas surrounding Nizamabad.

### Which one is more fuel-efficient?

Both vehicles offer competitive fuel efficiency. In real-world conditions around Nizamabad, we've found that the Exter's refined engine can sometimes edge out the Punch, especially in stop-and-go traffic, but official figures are quite close. ## Features and Interiors: Hyundai Exter vs Tata Punch

Step inside, and you'll immediately notice the differences in approach. Hyundai is known for its feature-rich cabins, and the Exter is no exception. It comes packed with segment-first features that truly enhance the driving experience.

The Hyundai Exter boasts an 8-inch touchscreen infotainment system with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, a digital instrument cluster, automatic climate control, a wireless charger, and a sunroof (a big hit with younger buyers!). But the real showstopper is the standard fitment of 6 airbags across all variants. This commitment to safety is something we at Prakash Hyundai strongly advocate for, and it gives the Exter a significant edge.

The Tata Punch also offers a respectable feature list, including a 7-inch touchscreen infotainment system, automatic climate control, and connected car tech on higher variants. Its interior design is rugged and practical, with good storage spaces. While it doesn't offer 6 airbags as standard across all variants, it has a 5-star Global NCAP safety rating, which speaks volumes about its structural integrity.

### Comfort and Space

Both cars offer decent space for four adults, with a fifth passenger being a squeeze on longer journeys. The Exter's cabin feels slightly more airy and premium thanks to its lighter colour themes and better fit-and-finish. The Punch, with its wider stance, can feel a bit more planted, and its rear seating is comfortable for average-sized adults. Boot space is also comparable, offering enough room for your weekly groceries or a weekend trip to Kuntala Waterfalls.

## Driving Dynamics and Ground Clearance

Both vehicles offer a decent amount of ground clearance, which is essential for navigating some of the uneven roads or unpaved paths we often encounter in and around Nizamabad. The Hyundai Exter offers 185mm of ground clearance, while the Tata Punch has 187mm. This slight difference is negligible in daily driving, and both handle speed breakers and minor off-road excursions with ease.

On the road, the Exter feels lighter and more agile, making it a joy to weave through city traffic. Its steering is light and responsive. The Punch, with its slightly heavier build, feels more planted and robust, especially at higher speeds. Its suspension is tuned to absorb bumps well, giving it a solid feel.
